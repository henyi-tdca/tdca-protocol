## 1. What is an "Encounter"

An **Encounter** is a **match event that arises naturally**, when, under **open information and open rules**, two parties **discover each other and move toward each other voluntarily (a two-way convergence)**.

One-line distinction:

| | Encounter | Matchmaking |
|---|---|---|
| Who acts | **Both parties** (voluntary, mutual) | **A third party** providing an active service |
| Nature | A **natural event** | An **active service** |
| Fee | **Free** (the encounter itself is not charged) | **Charged** (matchmaking service fee) |

> An **Encounter is not Matchmaking**: the two stand side by side and do **not** substitute for each other.

---

## 2. Why Encounters Matter

**An Encounter is the first step inside a "Scene".**

- **An Encounter presupposes a Scene** — a Scene is the precondition for an Encounter (no Scene, no Encounter).
- Conversely, **for a particular Encounter, a corresponding Scene may first be built** (set the Scene up on demand).

In plain terms: first make clear "on what occasion, with whom, and under which rules" the parties interact (that is the Scene); only then does an Encounter have its place.

---

## 3. Three Basic Rules

**Rule 1 · Keep them apart**
An Encounter and Matchmaking are **two different things** and do not substitute for each other. **Matchmaking is charged; the Encounter itself is not** — no "encounter entry fee" and no "encounter traffic fee".

**Rule 2 · Record only the fact**
An Encounter records only **"occurred / did not occur"**. **There is no "true encounter vs. fake encounter" distinction.** The reason: an Encounter is a **two-way convergence** (both sides act voluntarily), so there is no notion of "fake".

**Rule 3 · No skipping**
The flow has three steps: **Discovery → Encounter → Handshake**.
**Before the handshake, no configuration right may be invoked** — the boundary is enforced by the system, not left to goodwill.

---

## 4. Why It Is Designed This Way

1. **It happens only under open, checkable conditions** — open information and open rules: visible to all, traceable.
2. **It is two-way, not manufactured** — hence no "anti-fake" layer is needed.
3. **It protects participants** — without a handshake, nothing is released; the discovery stage yields only "leads", and exposure or citation scores may not serve as a basis.

---

## 5. FAQ

**Q: Is an Encounter charged?**
A: The Encounter itself is **free**. What is charged is "matchmaking service" (an active third-party service).

**Q: How do we know an Encounter "occurred"?**
A: Only "occurred / did not occur" is recorded; **no true/false judgment** is made.

**Q: Without a handshake, may we use the other side's configuration right?**
A: **No.** The flow "Discovery → Encounter → Handshake" must be completed; any invocation before the handshake is rejected.
