## Preamble: Four Pillars

The problem TDCA addresses is to make **institutions and technology isomorphic** in the age of artificial intelligence, so that collaboration between machines, and between people and machines, is **trustworthy, auditable and verifiable**. The four pillars follow.

**One. Why — the scenario objective function**
Solve the isomorphism between institutions and technology so that interaction among agents is trustworthy and safe. The approach is two-way: internalise institutional constraints into technical design constraints (audit, smart contracts, circuit-breakers), and externalise technical runtime states into institution-readable audit evidence.
**Trustworthy** means behaviour is predictable, process is auditable, results are verifiable; **safe** means the payoff from jailbreaking is never positive.

**Two. Who and how — frictionless application**
Let every subject (people, agents, organisations) use these rules **without friction**. Institutions should become a built-in default, like a network protocol: **almost imperceptible when you comply, blocked the moment you cross the line.**

**Three. With what — resource integration**
Three things must combine: cross-disciplinary knowledge, real data and computing power. Difference is not an obstacle but a source: the heterogeneity of different subjects is precisely the mother lode of new utility.

**Four. What it looks like — positive-sum utility**
The outcome must be positive-sum: compliant parties face lower friction and earn higher trust; those who cross the line face costs exceeding gains, so a rational actor will not jailbreak; and the system as a whole avoids the tragedy of the commons.

## Chapter I General Provisions

**Article 1 (Observability)**
Every configuration act must leave an auditable digital trace — who initiated it, what the subject matter is, in which scenario, what utility is expected, and what the tax data is. **An unobservable act is void.**
*This article guards against: being unable to reconstruct the facts afterwards, with no one to hold to account.*

**Article 2 (Positive sum)**
Every configuration must verify that the sum of the parties' expected gains is positive. **Zero-sum and negative-sum configurations are void automatically** and trigger a circuit-breaker.
*This article guards against: one side gaining and the other losing, yet the deal being counted as concluded.*

**Article 3 (Self-evidence)**
The initiating party must provide utility self-evidence, stating the minimum hard indicators for production, consumption, interaction volume and tax anchoring. **Those who cannot provide it may not initiate.**
*This article guards against: asserting value without giving grounds.*

**Article 4 (Protection of the essential-need line)**
Resources that sustain basic survival and core means of production **do not enter market-based configuration**; only ownership is retained.
*This article guards against: trading what should not be traded.*

**Article 5 (Ownership isolation)**
**Transfers of ownership are prohibited** within the protocol network; only usage rights and configuration rights may be configured.
*This article guards against: doing "buying and selling ownership" under the name of "configuration".*

**Article 6 (Negative-space circuit-breaker)**
The moment a claim touches a hard constraint, **the circuit-breaker fires automatically**: trading is suspended and the matter moves to human review or sandbox testing. **No party may bypass the circuit-breaker.**
*This article guards against: pushing ahead while knowing the line has been crossed.*

**Article 7 (Scenario boundary)**
Configuration rights are subdivided by scenario. A micro-innovation need only satisfy the constraints **within its target scenario**, not across all scenarios.
*This article guards against: making a small change carry the compliance burden of every scenario, thereby stifling innovation.*

**Article 8 (Ownership layer)**
The confirmation, alteration and pledging of ownership **take place only at the statutory trading venue**.
*This article guards against: title moving privately, with nothing to check afterwards.*

**Article 9 (Usage-right layer)**
Usage rights are configured automatically by smart contract; **the smallest unit is the utility function of a single scenario**.
*This article guards against: rights granularity so coarse that configuration cannot be precise per scenario.*

**Article 10 (Configuration-right layer)**
A configuration right is a **collaboration-scheduling right, not a right of possession**. Its price is set by the scenario's utility weights.
*This article guards against: mistaking a "scheduling right" for "ownership", and so creating disguised possession.*

## Appendix 3: Nature and boundaries

- This Constitution sets **hard constraints**: it may not be changed by version upgrade, only interpreted.
- No higher norm exists above this Constitution; below it are regulations, specifications, detailed rules and operating guides.
- Where an article conflicts with another instrument, **this Constitution prevails**.
